# bend-spike — Does Bend 2 complement the physics simulation stack? (spike closing report, 2026-09-18)

> This is the original README of the spike, kept as a historical record when the repository was reorganised for Phase 3
> (2026-09-19). The current README is at the root.

Research spike (not production), 2026-09-18. Question: whether Bend 2 (bendlang/bend, 2.0.6) can complement JAX float64 in the **symbolic and verification layer** of fusion tools (configuration optimisation, plasma control, V&V of safety codes), without touching the numerical layer.

## Thesis

Every physics code has a hot layer (grids, linear algebra, time integration: float64, regular) where Bend 2 does not compete, and a symbolic layer (term rewriting, kernel generation, discrete supervisory logic, exact oracles, provenance) where its dependent types and compile-time proofs could contribute. 5 hypotheses from the author and 4 of our own were audited, and a complete PoC of the strongest one was built.

## Two rounds

- **v1** (`docs/`, `heat/`, `env/`): Phases 0 and 1 with shortcuts (semantics over `Nat`, schemes restricted to non-negative weights, bignum tested but not proven). It served to discover where the holes were.
- **v2** (`v2/`): the same two phases redone on a proven numerical base, with the specifications designed and reviewed by instantiation before proving. **43 laws, 43 proven**, everything that is not I/O or float under law. Design and review: `v2/docs/phase0-v2-design.md`; findings: `v2/docs/phase1-v2-findings.md`; reproduce: `py -3.14 v2/heat/run.py` → `all gates and checks ok: True`.
- **Phase 2** (`v2/seq/`): tokamak discharge sequencer with machine-protection interlocks. **12 laws, 12 proven**, including the theorem "no event trace takes the system out of the safe set"; finite certificate by computation; reference model loaded from Python (JS loader) and differential testing with Hypothesis against an implementation with planted bugs. Design: `v2/docs/phase2-design.md`; findings: `v2/docs/phase2-findings.md`; reproduce: `py -3.14 v2/seq/run.py` → `all gates and checks ok: True`.

## What was tested

- **Phase 0** (`docs/phase0-reassessment.md`): re-evaluation with real knowledge of the language, audit of each hypothesis, ranking, choice of PoC, environment verification with real hello-worlds (`env/`, `bash env/check_env.sh`: 11 checks).
- **Phase 1** (`docs/phase1-findings.md`, `heat/`): 1D heat equation. Expression IR in Bend with exact semantics, simplifier with a **correctness law proven by induction**, two stencil certificates **proven by computation** (sum of weights = denominator, which is the CFL condition; symmetry), exactness over linear profiles proven for all `a, b`; JAX kernel emitted from the simplified tree; exact oracle from the **same** tree (plan A over `Nat`, 13 steps; plan B with our own bignum, 200 steps); comparison against JAX float64/float32 and against the analytical solution. Reproduce: `py -3.14 heat/run.py`.

## What was closed (v2)

- **Proven numerical base** (`v2/num/`, 32 laws): canonical integers `Z` with the full commutative ring (22 laws), big naturals as bit lists with the proven adder (5), big integers as `p − n` with `add`/`neg`/`scale` proven via `to_z` (5). Reusable for any discrete layer.
- **IR linear by construction** over `Z`, with the stencil written as the physics (`D·u_i + p·(u_{i−1} − 2u_i + u_{i+1})`), a proven normaliser to coefficients (`lin_sound`), consistency and symmetry certificates, exactness over linear profiles for all `a, b`, sum of weights = `D` for **every** dyadic `r`, and the stability certificate `weights_nonneg` with a negative test (`r = 3/4`) that the checker rejects.
- **End-to-end oracle under law**: evaluation, time step and N steps over big integers proven equal to the specification in `Z`. Only text ↔ bits and Python remain outside.
- **Numerics**: `r = 1/4` and `r = 3/8`, 13 and 200 steps: the oracle matches `Fraction` exactly; float64 and float32 stay within ≈ 1 ulp of the exact value (the scheme is a convex combination, and that is proven); the distance to the analytical solution is discretisation and is the same for JAX and the oracle.
- Six proof files, six prover agents, 1–4 checker iterations each, no false law in the prior instantiation.

## What was closed (Phase 2)

- **Verified sequencer** (`v2/seq/seq.bend`, 7 phases, 15 events, 6 invariants): gas or plasma only with vacuum and field, heating only in flat-top with density, solenoid and valve only in their phases, flat-top and watchdog bounded. `traces_safe` proves that they hold for every trace and every value of the counters; disruption and abort lead to the safe state from every state. 1593 lines of proof (two agents, 2 and 4 iterations; the glue by hand).
- **Certificate by computation**: 1792 states × 18 events decided by the checker in 5.5 s with `{==}`; the exhaustive runtime instantiation (7168 × 18) found no false law before proving. The laws forced the design to be fixed twice (watchdog outside `Idle`; `RampDown` is a phase with plasma).
- **Differential testing**: the Bend model as a JSON service in node (~1 ms per query); Hypothesis against a Python implementation with two planted bugs: both found with minimal traces (5 and 7 events) and both oracles (trajectory and Bend invariants over the production states) firing. The pure random generator does **not** reach the deep bug in 3000 traces; the guided one does in 200 queries; the proof covers it without generating anything.
- Negative test: the variant with the `RampDown` bug is rejected by the checker with the explicit counterexample.
- **Phase 2b, technical debt settled** (`v2/seq3/`, `v2/docs/phase2b-technical-debt.md`): the sequencer redesigned with finite control + commands to the counters. The same guarantees with **323 lines of proof instead of 1593** (reflection over a certificate by computation of 448 × 18 × 4 cells, plus a generic lemma for the counters), and a certificate that the new model behaves exactly like the previous one (7168 × 18). Reproduce: `py -3.14 v2/seq3/run.py`.

## What was closed (v1)

- All 4 laws: `bend heat/PROOF.bend` → `All terms check.`
- The circuit symbolic → generated → numerical → verified, end to end, on Windows without a native toolchain.
- JAX float64 vs exact oracle: **0** at 13 steps (everything fits in 53 bits), 2.7e-16 relative at 200 steps (≈ 1 ulp; bound N·ε = 4.4e-14). Float32: ≈ 1 ulp as well. Against the analytical solution: 1.3e-3 and 6.8e-5, identical for JAX and oracle, explained by the discretisation (the oracle matches the closed form of the discrete scheme to 1e-10).
- The bignum oracle matches Python's `fractions.Fraction` bit for bit at 200 steps.
- In Phase 0, a toy interlock with two proven invariants (83 lines of proof); the checker found an error in the specification and gave the counterexample.

## What did not close (or is left out by design)

- Nothing about floats: `F32` is axiomatic, there is no `F64`. The laws speak of the exact scheme, not of the kernel compiled by XLA. The boundary is: exact in Bend, float in JAX, bridge through exact dyadic inputs and numerical comparison.
- Non-linear terms: outside the linear IR by design; going in means a polynomial IR with its normal form and its laws (another project).
- `Z` is specification, not engine: its unary arithmetic cannot be executed beyond tiny values (it blew the JS stack with 3 steps). The engine is `BigZ`; the law that links them is what gets run.
- No native backend or GPU on this machine (nor on any Windows): everything ran on the JS backend, 0.2 s per check and 1.8 s per run of the 200-step oracle with 600-bit numerators.
- Cost in code: v2 has 1666 lines of Bend (528 program, 176 laws, 962 proofs) against 41 of Python + sympy + `Fraction` for the same pipeline with no guarantee at all. v1 had 554.
- Resolved in v2 what v1 left open: `Nat < 2^48` (proven bit bignum), no sign (proven canonical `Z`), "CFL by saturation" (explicit certificate with negative test), unproven bignum (5 laws via `to_nat`).

## Premises of the brief that turned out false

Bend 2 does not run on interaction nets (BendRT runtime: static fork-join, no work stealing; unbalanced trees are its worst case). It has no synthesis. It is not "much slower than C" sequentially (0.8–1.5×), although it only has `F32`.

## Honest conclusion

Bend 2 contributes nothing to the numerical layer and does not change what can be simulated. It contributes, today, in two niches, both solid after v2: symbolic transformations and certificates with a machine-checked guarantee (Phase 1), and supervisory logic with invariants proven for every trace, usable as a reference model against the real implementation (Phase 2). The cost in code is ~10× relative to Python without proofs and ~40× with them; the language's poor arithmetic (`Nat < 2^48`, no sign, no rationals) is solved with a proven base of our own of ~1400 lines that is then reused. What v2 showed about the process: when the specification is designed for the checker and reviewed by instantiation before proving, the proofs are distributable mechanical work (43 laws, 0 false, 1–4 iterations per file). Bend does not prevent the AI from making mistakes when writing; it guarantees that no future mistake in the layer under laws passes silently. The dominant risk is the language itself: one year old, one vendor, no native Windows or scientific ecosystem. Realistic use: specification and reference model written in Bend, exported as text, JS or C; never as a dependency of the production pipeline.
